# Personal Word user guide

The step-by-step guide that used to live in the README. For the short version, see the [README](../README.md).

## A short workflow

1. **Start a project.** On the Projects screen, choose **Start new project**, then **Chicago**. The template sets 12-point serif type, double spacing, one-inch margins, and a title page. Blank and Screenplay are offered too; Essay and Story are hidden from the menus, and projects already created from them still open.
2. **Write in sections.** The sidebar lists the paper's sections. Click one to open it, and add more with the **+** button beside Manuscript. Drag a section, a fragment, a source, a note or a sidebar group to reorder it, or focus it and press Option+Up or Option+Down to move it one place. The toolbar's **Page view** stacks the section as pages and splits a paragraph across a page break the way Word does, keeping at least two lines on each page.
3. **Add a source.** Open **Research** in the sidebar. Paste a DOI, ISBN, PubMed or PMC link, or PMID (for example "PMID 33301246"; a bare number is not read as a PMID) or web address and choose **Look up**, choose **Enter manually**, or import a local Zotero **CSL JSON** or **BibTeX** (.bib) export. Review the preview before adding sources. The panel shows the Chicago note and bibliography entry as you type.
4. **Cite it.** Put the cursor where the note belongs and choose **Cite here**. A numbered footnote appears in the text and at the bottom of the page. New projects shorten repeated citations automatically; existing projects keep that option off until enabled. Choose a Short title and override a note to Always full or Always short when needed. **Capture quote** saves a passage with its page and can insert both quotation and citation together.
5. **Take a snapshot.** Before a big revision, choose **Snapshot** at the top right and name it. **Snapshots & history** lets you compare versions side by side and restore a section or a single paragraph.
6. **Export.** Choose **Export** at the top right (next to **Snapshot**), or press Cmd+Shift+E, or pick "Export..." in the command palette. The menu lists Word (.docx) for an editable file with real footnotes, PDF, Markdown, Markdown with images (.zip), Plain text, **Export settings...** (opens Settings at Export document) and Complete project (.json). Arrow keys move through it, Enter picks, Escape closes it. Markdown and Plain text include the whole project, including notes, fragments, and research notes. **Markdown with images (.zip)** adds the pictures and a checksum manifest, and can be imported again.

**Bibliography.** The Research part of the sidebar lists **Bibliography** and then every source, with research notes under **Notes**. Bibliography appears only when it means something: for a project started from the **Chicago** template, or once the project has a source or a footnote that cites one; otherwise the entry is hidden. The page is read-only: choose the entry order (Alphabetical by author, Order first cited, By source type) and whether to include uncited sources. The choice is saved with the project and used by Word, PDF, Markdown and plain-text export. **Read manuscript** ends with the same Bibliography.

**PDFs.** Attach a PDF (up to 25 MB) to a source in Research, or press **Upload PDF** in the Research panel to keep one that belongs to no source; it gets its own PDFs list. Open either beside the draft. PDFs are stored in the project folder and are never fetched from the internet; they are not carried inside the project's `.json` archive or the Markdown bundle (see [Limitations](limitations.md)).

**Headers.** The three sidebar parts (Manuscript, Fragments, Research) are headers, and you can add more beside Manuscript: press **Add header** under the list (or right-click empty space, or use the command palette), give it a name, then add sections inside it with its "+". Right-click a header you added to **Rename** or **Delete** it. When Settings > General > Palette is Color, every header's right-click menu ends with a row of colour squares: click one (or use the arrow keys and Enter) to recolour that header's icons in this project only; a header you added starts with Manuscript's colour. Deleting a header moves its sections to Manuscript, and a snapshot is kept first.

**Open a section or a fragment beside the draft.** Cmd+click its sidebar row, or use the **Open beside** button that appears on hover or keyboard focus. Drag the divider to change the split; the width ratio is remembered, and each pane edits its own entry, so you can type in both. Opening or closing a split keeps the left pane's undo history and scroll position, and both panes line their pages up under matching headers. With a split already open, **Open beside** (or Cmd/Ctrl+click on a row) replaces the pane you were last working in, not always the right one; if the entry is already showing in the other pane, focus just moves there. With no split open it opens a new right pane as before. A PDF always opens in the right pane.

**Click the blank page.** In page or continuous view, click the grey paper beside or below the text to put the caret at the nearest sensible spot: a side margin goes to the start or end of the line level with the click, and below the last line goes to the end of the document. Shift+click in the margin extends the selection. The ruler, footnote markers, page gaps and ordinary text behave as before. (Clicking blank paper in a split pane that is not focused is not yet confirmed.)

**Interface font and added fonts.** In **Settings > General**, **Interface font** sets the font for the whole interface; the logo stays Futura. **Add font...** takes one or more `.ttf` or `.otf` files (one family may have several styles: Regular, Bold, Italic, Bold italic); each family is listed with its styles and a **Remove** button. Added fonts and the fonts installed on your Mac both appear in the Interface font menu and in the toolbar's Font family menu, once per family. Added fonts are kept with the app, not inside a project (see [Limitations](limitations.md)). **Add font...** sits on the same row as the Interface font menu, with no file box showing, in project Settings and in the Settings opened from Projects; **Remove** is red. The toolbar's **Font family** menu starts with a **Recent** section (up to five fonts you picked from the menu, newest first, each in its own face) and a grey line, then the whole list. It is empty until you pick a font. Choosing the font that is already current does not move it to the top (see [Limitations](limitations.md)).

**Side ruler.** Turn on **Side ruler** in Page setup (it is its own switch; **Ruler** controls only the top ruler, and a project that had Ruler on before now has both on). In page view a second ruler with inch numbers runs down the left of every page, with the top and bottom margins shaded. Drag the top or bottom arrow, or focus one and press Up or Down (Shift for bigger steps, Home and End for the limits), to change the margin; the top ruler moves with it. It is hidden in continuous view, in focus mode, and while the sidebar floats. CLAIMED: Chromium only.

**Text-only notes.** Footnotes that cite no source are listed in Research > **Notes**, under your notes, with **Insert footnote at cursor** and the same Used in and Go to buttons. **Delete footnote** (red) asks "Delete this text-only footnote?" and names the section that holds it. If you confirm, the app first takes a snapshot called "Before deleting a footnote", then removes the note and its numbered marker from whichever section holds it, and the remaining footnotes renumber. A footnote that cites a source cannot be deleted this way (use **Unlink source** first). To undo, open Snapshots & history and restore "Before deleting a footnote". CLAIMED: Chromium only.

**Sources and where they are used.** An open source in Research reads in three parts: the source fields, the **Citation** previews with **Cite here**, **Add quote** and **Delete source**, and **Used in**, which counts the footnotes and quotes and shows, for each footnote, which section holds it ("in <section>") with a **Go to** button that opens that section with the footnote selected. If one footnote appears in several entries, only the first is reported. Less-used fields (Short title, Edition, DOI, URL, the manual overrides and the attached PDF with **Open beside**) are under **More details**, and the repeat-citation options are under **Citation rules** below the source list. Lookup needs an internet connection; everything else works offline. The Research panel slides in when you click Research, but not when you open it from the keyboard or the palette.

**Settings strip.** A row of section names under "Project settings" stays at the top as you scroll; click one to jump there. The section in view is bold and blue.

**Fragments.** Select text and choose the scissor (**Keep as fragment**) to set it aside; with nothing selected the scissor, and **Comment** beside it, act on the paragraph the cursor is in. A fragment has no restore strip: to move its words into a section, open the fragment beside the draft (Cmd+click its row, or **Open beside** in its right-click menu) and copy and paste. The Fragments panel's **Append** action still adds a fragment to a section.

**Ruler and top gap.** Open **Page setup** (in More on a narrow window) and press **Ruler** to show a ruler above the page (grey bands outside the margins show where the text ends). In page view the ruler is pinned in a full-width grey strip above the pages, so it never sits on the paper and the pages scroll beneath it; clicking the strip does nothing to the caret. With the sidebar floating, the strip sits beneath the sidebar. Drag either triangle to change the page margin (both sides move together; the same margin your Word and PDF exports use, from 1/4 to 2 inches), or focus a triangle and use the arrow keys (Shift for bigger steps, Home and End for the limits). In continuous view a small handle also appears at the top of the text: drag it, or use Up and Down, to change the space above the first line. That gap is only on screen; exports ignore it. Cmd+Z does not undo a ruler drag.

**Sidebar look.** The section you are in is bold and the rest of its list stays dim until you point at a row, focus it or open it. Dragging a sidebar edge or the split divider shows no coloured line; the cursor changes instead. The word count is only at the bottom of the page.

**Line rail.** In list view each Manuscript, Fragments and Research group ends in a rail of short lines, one per item, in the same order as the rows. Click a line (or focus it and press Enter or Space) to open that item, and in Read mode to scroll to that section. The lines do not reorder anything: drag a row, or press Option+Up or Option+Down on it. Each line is one extra Tab stop after its group's rows.

**Find in project.** The field at the top of the sidebar filters its rows (Cmd+K focuses it; there is no hint written in the field). When it has text, a small **x** appears at its right to clear it and keep typing; Escape does the same without leaving the field.

**Floating sidebar and research.** In **Settings > Sidebar**, turn on **Floating sidebar and research** (off by default). When the page has wide margins, the sidebar then floats in the left margin as plain text on the page, without its own background, and the **Research** heading opens a small citation card in the right margin instead of the side panel. It never covers your text: if the window or the page is too narrow, you are in a split view, or a page such as Settings or Snapshots & history is showing, the usual sidebar and Research panel appear instead. If you widen the window while the panel is open, the card takes over again once there is room.

**The citation card.** Open Research (or click a footnote number in the text). Paste a DOI, ISBN or web address and choose **Look up**, or choose **Enter manually**; check the details, then choose **Cite here** to add a footnote where the cursor is. The card then lets you set the page and note form, **Add another** or finish with **Done**. After Cite here the cursor moves to the new footnote's text, just as with the panel. **Open full research panel** at the bottom of the card switches to the side panel. Escape closes the card.

**Command palette.** Press Cmd+Shift+P to search every action the app owns (Save, Take snapshot, New section and so on, each showing its shortcut), jump to any section or fragment with "Go to ...", or open Research, Comments, Snapshots & history, Settings or Read. Type to filter, use Up and Down, press Enter to run, Escape to close; your cursor returns to where it was. You can change the shortcut in **Settings > Shortcuts**.

**Right-click a sidebar row** (or press Shift+F10 on it) for Open, Open beside, Rename, Move up, Move down and Delete; on a group heading it offers Collapse or Expand and Move.

The **Projects** screen has a search button that opens a field and filters the list by title. **Sort**, the reverse arrow and the list/cards toggle sit at the right end of the top bar, before the gear; your choices are remembered. Each row shows the title, then "Edited <date>" and "Added <date>". With no projects it offers Blank, Chicago, Screenplay and Import writing. **Start new project** opens a panel listing the templates, then **Import a file…** and **Recover from a project.json file…**. The **gear** at the right of the display row opens **Settings** without leaving Projects. You can also drag one `.txt`, `.md`, `.docx`, `.html` or `.zip` file onto the Projects screen to open the import preview, or choose **Import writing** for the same picker from the keyboard; Cancel creates nothing.

**Appearance:** in **Settings > General**, choose Appearance (System, Light or Dark) and Palette (Plain or Color). The sidebar options (reveal on hover, floating, and per project which of Read, Comments and History show) are together in **Settings > Sidebar**. Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+L switches between Light and Dark. System follows macOS live; Color adds section tints in the sidebar and colored buttons without changing the document area.

Set manuscript/daily word goals and a deadline in **Settings > Goals**. **Settings > Shortcuts** lists every shortcut the app binds and lets you change the ones it owns; menu hints follow your changes. The toolbar offers **Focus mode** (Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+F) and an optional local **Prose check** with a keyboard-accessible issue list.

The app saves as you type. **Export > Complete project (.json)** at the top right saves a complete copy of the project, which is useful as a backup. Prose check shows "N issues" ("1 issue" for one), with each rule's count in its row.

Everything new in wave 9 is CLAIMED, not verified: it has been checked in a browser and by unit tests, not yet in the real Mac app.

## Where your writing lives

Each project is stored in its own folder in your Mac's application-data area, under the name `com.folio.writing`. In **Settings**, choose **Show path** next to Storage location to see the exact folder. Each folder holds a SQLite database, which is the real copy, plus readable JSON and plain-text copies.

To back up, quit the app and copy the whole project folder somewhere else. The only time the app uses the internet is when you choose **Look up** for a citation. It sends the DOI or ISBN you entered to Crossref or Open Library (a PubMed or PMC link or PMID goes to NCBI, which may then lead to Crossref), or fetches the web page you named. A DOI found in that page’s metadata may trigger a follow-up request to Crossref. The optional Zotero refresh (off by default) talks only to Zotero on your own Mac.
